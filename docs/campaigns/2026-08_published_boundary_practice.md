# Published SFINCS boundary practice — and how ours differs

Both papers are in `refs/` (with Grimley's Supporting Information `.docx`). Extract text
with `pip install --target <scratch> pypdf`; **no PDF tooling exists in any env here**
(no poppler, no fitz). For the `.docx`: `zipfile` → `word/document.xml` → strip tags.

## Grimley et al. 2025 (WRR) — Hurricane Florence, the Carolinas

Authors include **Leijnse + Eilander** (SFINCS/Deltares) and **Luettich** (ADCIRC).

🔑 **THE BOUNDARY IS DRAWN, NOT GENERATED.** Verbatim from SI §S1:

> "Water level cells along the coastal boundary are **on average at the −15 m+NAVD88
> contour**, **specified using a modified shapefile of the National Hydrography Dataset
> (NHD) Area** which delineates the extent of rivers and water bodies."

The depth contour is **where it lands on average — an outcome, not the rule.** Figure S1
confirms hand curation: NHD Area "modified to improve the delineation of the estuaries and
sounds." ⇒ This is the direct opposite of our `mask_zmin = -10`, which is a *generative*
rule, and it is why they cannot get an inlet clamp: a curated water-body polygon does not
know or care how deep a scoured inlet is.

Other specifics:
- **SFINCS one-way coupled to ADCIRC**; 20-min storm tide from a validated Florence
  hindcast (Ratcliff 2022) at the downstream boundary.
- Storm tide extracted at **5,500+ ADCIRC points** (2.0 km spacing in estuaries/sounds/
  coastline, 5.0 km in the ocean); **341** interpolated onto the SFINCS boundary cells;
  only points **within 2.0 km** used ⇒ nothing extrapolated.
- **Both ADCIRC and SFINCS forced with the SAME wind field** (modified OWI, 0.05°/15-min)
  ⇒ boundary surge and interior wind are physically consistent. Ours are not (gauges + ERA5).
- ADCIRC is MSL → NAVD88 via a **NOAA VDatum correction raster**.
- At lateral edges: **outflow boundary cells** "where inter-basin flow might occur… where it
  might otherwise unrealistically pool," explicitly for "low-gradient areas at the coast."
- Initial coastal water level 0.25 m NAVD88 + a **24-hr startup "filling the bays and
  estuaries with water"** — worth checking our spin-up against, given the bay volume deficit.
- Performance: peak error **0.11 m**, RMSE **0.92 m** over 852 locations (89 gauges + 763
  HWMs). ⚠️ Not directly comparable to ours — their domain runs inland to 200 m NAVD88 with
  rainfall and runoff — but useful calibration: our `tide-shift` RMSE 0.466 is not the weak part.

## Leijnse et al. 2025 (Coastal Engineering 199, 104726) — waves in compound flooding

Same storm. Closer to our configuration:
- Forced from **GTSM**, statistically corrected by **Parker et al. (2023)** on tidal
  components, seasonality and NTR. Output points **~460 m apart alongshore**, applied at
  **approximately −10 m NAVD88** ⇒ **our `mask_zmin = -10` matches Deltares' own practice.**
  The depth is not our problem.
- Wavemakers at ~5 m depth at high tide.
- ⭐ **Parker et al. (2023) is the actionable lead**: we retired `phaselag_gtsm` because
  GTSM's tide is ~34% under-amplitude (×0.66 at 6 stations). That is exactly what Parker
  corrects. Check whether it covers the US East Coast for 2012.
- ⚠️ But on that same corrected product they report *"likely underestimation of the peak
  storm surge in GTSM, which is driven by local wind speeds of ERA5, in which the **peak
  wind speeds are underestimated**."* ⇒ corrected GTSM plausibly helps our tide phase and
  along-bay gradient, and **not** our surge peak.
  ⚠️ This is a claim about **peak TC winds**, not about the bay/ocean roughness reduction
  tested in [[reference_wind_forcing_investigation]] — it does not overturn that weakening.

## The gap that actually matters

| | them | us |
|---|---|---|
| boundary line | drawn from a curated polygon | generated by a depth threshold + 6 patch boxes |
| support points | 341 (Grimley) / ~460 m spacing (Leijnse) | **2**, 39.6 km apart |
| max extrapolation | 2.0 km | ~40 km |
| boundary depth | −15 m avg / −10 m | −10 m ✅ |

⇒ Most of our boundary pain — the Raritan inconsistency, the severity of the inlet clamp,
"linear interpolation between two outside points cannot produce an interior maximum," the
whole `mask==2` audit problem — is downstream of having **2 support points**. Placement is
fragile precisely because the boundary VALUE is only valid on the open coast.
